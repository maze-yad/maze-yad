Where it begins

Santa, the smile everyone knows

Before anything else, Christmas needs a face people recognise from across the street. Rosy cheeks, a beard like fresh snow and a hat slipping to one side.

Why we chose itHe's the universal welcome. Opening with Santa tells every visitor, young or old, what kind of night this is, so the local stories that follow feel like an invitation, not a puzzle.

From the oak woods

A squirrel in a Hawri scarf

She came down from the oak trees with the brightest bauble she could find, dropped it once in the excitement, and picked it right back up. Her Hawri scarf stayed wrapped twice against the cold.

Why we chose itThe Hawri is a Kurdish identity design, and we wanted it worn, not just displayed. On a playful forest animal the pattern feels friendly and close, like a scarf knitted by a grandmother.

On the mountain road

A bell you hear before you see

Holly in her mane, a Hawri scarf at her neck and a small brass bell that rings with every step through the snow.

Why we chose itHorses carried people and stories across these mountains long before there were roads. She stands for the journey home for the holidays, and her bell brings the jingle every Christmas needs.

From the mountain woods

Baru, the oak and its acorns

Three oak leaves and two acorns, tied together with a red Christmas ribbon like a small gift from the forest.

Why we chose itOak forests cover the mountains around us, and the baru has been gathered through the winters for generations. Tied with a Christmas bow, it brings the strength and patience of the oak into the season, rooted in this land and still giving.

The mountain partridge

The kew, our bird of the hills

Striped flanks, a black necklace and bright red feet. Its call carries across the slopes even in the coldest weeks.

Why we chose itThe kew lives in Kurdish songs and poems as much as on the hillsides. Christmas cards always have a bird on them, and we wanted this season to have one of its own singing along.

By the stove

And then, a glass of tea

Strong, sweet and steaming in a slim glass, the saucer clinking under a tiny spoon.

Why we chose itNo visit here ends without tea. It is the most everyday act of hospitality we know, and it says what Christmas Nights is really about: sitting together, staying a little longer and pouring one more glass.
